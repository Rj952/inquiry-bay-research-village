Inquiry Bay Research Village

Host’s guide

How to run the village with a group, in a workshop or online.

Open the village

What the village is

Inquiry Bay is a guided walk through the research process. It has ten stops, laid out in the order a real study is built: from what research is, through the problem, questions, literature, design, ethics, data and analysis, to a finished Proposal Blueprint.

At every stop, people gather key concepts, practise until every answer is right, reflect on their own research idea (at least 40 words), and collect a stamp. Each stop opens only when the one before it is complete.

It suits anyone new to research methods. People get the most from it when they bring a real research idea with them.

Before you start

  • Walk it yourself first. Add #preview to the end of the village address to open every stop for a look around. Progress made in preview mode is for testing only.
  • Ask people to bring a research idea. A rough topic is enough. Their reflections and Blueprint grow from it.
  • Check devices. It works on phones, tablets and computers. Headphones help with the narration. Each stop also has a written transcript.
  • Explain how saving works. Work is saved in each person’s own browser. It does not follow them to another device, and private or incognito windows may lose it. Remind people to use Download as Word or Save as PDF in My passport.
  • Decide on sharing. Sharing progress with the host is optional. People who want to share tap Share (optional) and enter a name or nickname. No one has to.

Discussion prompts for each stop

  1. Arrival Jetty: Think of a decision in your workplace that was made without evidence. What happened?
  2. Worldview Lookout: Do you lean towards numbers, stories, or both? Why might that be?
  3. Craft Market: Share your topic in one sentence. Can your partner help you narrow it twice?
  4. Fisherman’s Pier: Read your purpose and question aloud. Does your partner hear the same problem in both?
  5. Old Courthouse Library: What makes a source empirical? Show one you found.
  6. Boatyard: Why might a design that works in a large country be hard to use in a small island setting?
  7. Lighthouse: What is the biggest ethical risk in your study, and who holds power?
  8. Cookshop: Who will you pilot your instrument with, and what are you hoping to learn?
  9. Coffee Sorting Shed: Walk your partner through your analysis one step at a time.
  10. Proposal Pavilion: Which link in your alignment chain is weakest? What will you fix first?

Helpful features

  • Quick check: under each reflection, a short checklist shows what a strong answer usually includes. It runs on the device. It is a prompt, not a mark.
  • Worked example: each stop has an optional example from Andrea, a fictional teacher, so people can see what a good answer looks like.
  • Capstone check: after the Blueprint, people rate their work against eleven points before writing the full proposal.
  • Download my work: My passport saves the Blueprint, reflections and glossary as a Word file or a PDF.
  • Access: transcripts for all narration, an A+ button for larger text, full keyboard use, and dark mode that follows the device.
  • Use it like an app: people can add it to their home screen and use it with a patchy connection.

After the walk

  • Ask people to bring their downloaded Blueprint to the next meeting and swap it with a partner for feedback.
  • Use the Capstone check results to plan what to teach next: the points most people mark “Not yet” show where the group needs more support.
  • If people shared their progress, the admin page shows stamps and reflections for your group.
